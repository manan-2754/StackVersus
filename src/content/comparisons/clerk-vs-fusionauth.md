## Detailed Breakdown

### Clerk

_Drop-in authentication and user management for web apps_

**Pros:**

- Polished prebuilt components
- Organizations and multi-tenancy
- Excellent Next.js integration

**Cons:**

- Less flexible outside JavaScript frameworks
- Costs rise with active users

---

### FusionAuth

_Developer-focused auth you can self-host_

**Pros:**

- Free self-hosted Community edition
- Runs anywhere including on-premises
- Broad protocol support

**Cons:**

- Not open source
- Self-hosting adds operational work

---

## Key Differences

- **Positioning:** Clerk — drop-in authentication and user management for web apps. FusionAuth — developer-focused auth you can self-host.
- Both share the same licensing model (proprietary), so the decision comes down to features and workflow fit.
- **Deployment:** Clerk — managed cloud. FusionAuth — self-hosted or managed cloud.
- **Pricing:** Clerk — free tier with paid plans. FusionAuth — free Community edition; paid plans.
- **Signature strength:** Clerk — polished prebuilt components. FusionAuth — free self-hosted Community edition.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Clerk better than FusionAuth?

It depends on your requirements. Clerk is a strong fit for React and Next.js apps wanting prebuilt auth UI, while FusionAuth suits teams wanting full control with self-hosted auth.

### Is Clerk free to use?

Yes, you can start with Clerk for free. Pricing model: Free tier with paid plans.

### Is FusionAuth free to use?

Yes, you can start with FusionAuth for free. Pricing model: Free Community edition; paid plans.

### Can I self-host Clerk or FusionAuth?

Clerk is offered as a managed service: managed cloud. FusionAuth can be self-hosted. Deployment options: self-hosted or managed cloud.

### What are the main drawbacks of Clerk and FusionAuth?

Clerk: less flexible outside JavaScript frameworks; costs rise with active users. FusionAuth: not open source; self-hosting adds operational work.
