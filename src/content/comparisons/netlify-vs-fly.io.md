# Netlify vs Fly.io: Head-to-Head Comparison

## Quick Verdict

> Netlify is the better pick for Jamstack sites and framework-agnostic frontends. Fly.io is the better pick for globally distributed apps running in many regions.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Netlify

_Platform for deploying modern web projects_

**Pros:**

- Simple Git-based deploys
- Built-in forms and serverless functions
- Framework-agnostic

**Cons:**

- Bandwidth and build minutes add up
- Less Next.js-specific optimization than Vercel

---

### Fly.io

_Platform for running full-stack apps close to users_

**Pros:**

- Runs apps in many regions
- Lightweight Firecracker microVMs
- Good for latency-sensitive apps

**Cons:**

- Steeper learning curve than simpler PaaS
- More operational responsibility

---

## Key Differences

- **Positioning:** Netlify — platform for deploying modern web projects. Fly.io — platform for running full-stack apps close to users.
- Both share the same licensing model (proprietary), so the decision comes down to features and workflow fit.
- Netlify can be started for free, while Fly.io requires a paid plan. Fly.io pricing: pay-as-you-go usage pricing.
- **Signature strength:** Netlify — simple Git-based deploys. Fly.io — runs apps in many regions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Netlify better than Fly.io?

It depends on your requirements. Netlify is a strong fit for Jamstack sites and framework-agnostic frontends, while Fly.io suits globally distributed apps running in many regions.

### Is Netlify free to use?

Yes, you can start with Netlify for free. Pricing model: Free tier with paid plans.

### Is Fly.io free to use?

Fly.io does not have a permanent free plan. Pricing model: Pay-as-you-go usage pricing.

### Can I self-host Netlify or Fly.io?

Netlify is offered as a managed service: managed cloud. Fly.io is offered as a managed service: managed cloud.

### What are the main drawbacks of Netlify and Fly.io?

Netlify: bandwidth and build minutes add up; less Next.js-specific optimization than Vercel. Fly.io: steeper learning curve than simpler PaaS; more operational responsibility.
