## Detailed Breakdown

### Datadog

_Monitoring and security platform for cloud apps_

**Pros:**

- Hundreds of integrations
- Unified metrics, logs, APM and security
- Polished dashboards

**Cons:**

- Costs can escalate quickly
- Complex per-product pricing

---

### Elastic Observability

_Observability built on the Elastic Stack_

**Pros:**

- Powerful log search
- Unified logs, metrics and APM
- Self-managed or Elastic Cloud

**Cons:**

- Cluster management overhead when self-hosted
- Resource-intensive at scale

## Key Differences

- **Positioning:** Datadog — monitoring and security platform for cloud apps. Elastic Observability — observability built on the Elastic Stack.
- Licensing differs: Datadog is proprietary while Elastic Observability is open source.
- **Deployment:** Datadog — managed cloud. Elastic Observability — self-hosted or managed cloud.
- **Pricing:** Datadog — free tier with paid plans. Elastic Observability — free open source; paid managed cloud.
- **Signature strength:** Datadog — hundreds of integrations. Elastic Observability — powerful log search.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Datadog better than Elastic Observability?

It depends on your requirements. Datadog is a strong fit for organizations wanting one platform for metrics, logs and traces, while Elastic Observability suits teams already using Elasticsearch for logs.

### Is Datadog free to use?

Yes, you can start with Datadog for free. Pricing model: Free tier with paid plans.

### Is Elastic Observability free to use?

Yes, you can start with Elastic Observability for free. Pricing model: Free open source; paid managed cloud.

### Can I self-host Datadog or Elastic Observability?

Datadog is offered as a managed service: managed cloud. Elastic Observability can be self-hosted. Deployment options: self-hosted or managed cloud.

### What are the main drawbacks of Datadog and Elastic Observability?

Datadog: costs can escalate quickly; complex per-product pricing. Elastic Observability: cluster management overhead when self-hosted; resource-intensive at scale.
