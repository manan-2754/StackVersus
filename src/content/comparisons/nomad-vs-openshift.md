# Nomad vs OpenShift: Head-to-Head Comparison

## Quick Verdict

> Nomad is the better pick for mixed workloads including containers, VMs and binaries. OpenShift is the better pick for enterprises needing supported Kubernetes with developer tooling.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Nomad

_Flexible workload orchestrator by HashiCorp_

**Pros:**

- Single binary with simple operations
- Runs containers and non-containerized apps
- Integrates with Consul and Vault

**Cons:**

- BSL license
- Smaller ecosystem than Kubernetes

---

### OpenShift

_Enterprise Kubernetes platform by Red Hat_

**Pros:**

- Enterprise support
- Integrated CI/CD and developer console
- Security-focused defaults

**Cons:**

- Expensive subscriptions
- Opinionated platform

---

## Key Differences

- **Positioning:** Nomad — flexible workload orchestrator by HashiCorp. OpenShift — enterprise Kubernetes platform by Red Hat.
- Licensing differs: Nomad is source-available while OpenShift is open-core.
- **Deployment:** Nomad — self-hosted. OpenShift — self-hosted or managed cloud.
- Nomad can be started for free, while OpenShift requires a paid plan. OpenShift pricing: custom enterprise pricing.
- **Signature strength:** Nomad — single binary with simple operations. OpenShift — enterprise support.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Nomad better than OpenShift?

It depends on your requirements. Nomad is a strong fit for mixed workloads including containers, VMs and binaries, while OpenShift suits enterprises needing supported Kubernetes with developer tooling.

### Is Nomad free to use?

Yes, you can start with Nomad for free. Pricing model: Free community edition; paid enterprise.

### Is OpenShift free to use?

OpenShift does not have a permanent free plan. Pricing model: Custom enterprise pricing.

### Can I self-host Nomad or OpenShift?

Nomad can be self-hosted. Deployment options: self-hosted. OpenShift can be self-hosted. Deployment options: self-hosted or managed cloud.

### What are the main drawbacks of Nomad and OpenShift?

Nomad: BSL license; smaller ecosystem than Kubernetes. OpenShift: expensive subscriptions; opinionated platform.
