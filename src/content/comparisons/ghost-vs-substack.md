# Ghost vs Substack: Head-to-Head Comparison

## Quick Verdict
> Choose Substack if you want a zero-cost, plug-and-play solution with a built-in audience network. Choose Ghost if you prioritize brand ownership, advanced customization, and lower long-term costs at scale.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Ghost
*The professional publishing platform for creators*

**Pros:**
- Full ownership of data and audience
- Advanced design and theme customization
- No transaction fees on paid subscriptions
- Robust API and integration ecosystem

**Cons:**
- Steeper learning curve for setup
- Requires monthly hosting fees
- No built-in discovery network

---

### Substack
*A home for your writing and paid subscriptions*

**Pros:**
- Completely free to start
- Built-in discovery and recommendation network
- Extremely simple setup and interface
- Integrated payment processing

**Cons:**
- 10% platform fee on all revenue
- Limited design and branding flexibility
- Platform lock-in makes migration difficult

---

## Key Differences
- Ownership: Ghost allows you to own your platform and data, while Substack acts as a hosted service where you are a tenant.
- Pricing: Ghost uses a fixed monthly fee, whereas Substack takes a 10% cut of your subscription revenue.
- Discovery: Substack provides a built-in network to help you find readers, while Ghost requires you to handle your own marketing and SEO.
- Customization: Ghost offers full theme control and custom domains, while Substack keeps a standardized look and feel across all newsletters.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I move my subscribers from Substack to Ghost?
Yes, both platforms allow you to export your subscriber list as a CSV file, making it relatively easy to migrate your audience.

### Which platform is cheaper for a large newsletter?
Ghost is generally cheaper at scale because you pay a flat monthly fee, whereas Substack's 10% fee becomes very expensive as your revenue grows.

### Do I need technical skills to use Ghost?
If you use Ghost(Pro) managed hosting, you do not need technical skills; however, self-hosting Ghost requires basic server management knowledge.
