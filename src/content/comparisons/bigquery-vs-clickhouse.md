# BigQuery vs ClickHouse: Head-to-Head Comparison

## Quick Verdict

> BigQuery is the better pick for serverless analytics in Google Cloud. ClickHouse is the better pick for real-time analytics on large event datasets.

## Detailed Breakdown

### BigQuery

_Google Cloud’s serverless data warehouse_

**Pros:**

- Fully serverless
- Built-in ML with BigQuery ML
- Free monthly query tier

**Cons:**

- On-demand pricing per bytes scanned
- Google Cloud centric

---

### ClickHouse

_Open-source column-oriented OLAP database_

**Pros:**

- Extremely fast aggregations
- Open source
- Managed ClickHouse Cloud option

**Cons:**

- Joins and updates are less ergonomic
- Requires operational tuning

---

## Key Differences

- **Positioning:** BigQuery — Google Cloud’s serverless data warehouse. ClickHouse — open-source column-oriented OLAP database.
- Licensing differs: BigQuery is proprietary while ClickHouse is open source.
- **Deployment:** BigQuery — managed cloud. ClickHouse — self-hosted or managed cloud.
- **Pricing:** BigQuery — free tier, then usage-based pricing. ClickHouse — free open source; paid managed cloud.
- **Signature strength:** BigQuery — fully serverless. ClickHouse — extremely fast aggregations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is BigQuery better than ClickHouse?

It depends on your requirements. BigQuery is a strong fit for serverless analytics in Google Cloud, while ClickHouse suits real-time analytics on large event datasets.

### Is BigQuery free to use?

Yes, you can start with BigQuery for free. Pricing model: Free tier, then usage-based pricing.

### Is ClickHouse free to use?

Yes, you can start with ClickHouse for free. Pricing model: Free open source; paid managed cloud.

### Can I self-host BigQuery or ClickHouse?

BigQuery is offered as a managed service: managed cloud. ClickHouse can be self-hosted. Deployment options: self-hosted or managed cloud.

### What are the main drawbacks of BigQuery and ClickHouse?

BigQuery: on-demand pricing per bytes scanned; Google Cloud centric. ClickHouse: joins and updates are less ergonomic; requires operational tuning.
