## Detailed Breakdown

### Sentry

_Application monitoring and error tracking_

**Pros:**

- Rich stack traces and context
- Performance tracing and session replay
- SDKs for most languages

**Cons:**

- Event quotas require tuning
- Self-hosting is resource-heavy

---

### Honeybadger

_Error, uptime and check-in monitoring for small teams_

**Pros:**

- Errors, uptime and cron monitoring together
- Simple pricing
- Developer-friendly support

**Cons:**

- Smaller feature set than Sentry
- Fewer integrations

---

## Key Differences

- **Positioning:** Sentry — application monitoring and error tracking. Honeybadger — error, uptime and check-in monitoring for small teams.
- Licensing differs: Sentry is source-available while Honeybadger is proprietary.
- **Deployment:** Sentry — self-hosted or managed cloud. Honeybadger — managed cloud.
- **Pricing:** Sentry — free tier with paid plans. Honeybadger — free tier with paid plans.
- **Signature strength:** Sentry — rich stack traces and context. Honeybadger — errors, uptime and cron monitoring together.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Sentry better than Honeybadger?

It depends on your requirements. Sentry is a strong fit for developers wanting detailed error and performance monitoring, while Honeybadger suits small teams on Ruby, Elixir, PHP and JavaScript stacks.

### Is Sentry free to use?

Yes, you can start with Sentry for free. Pricing model: Free tier with paid plans.

### Is Honeybadger free to use?

Yes, you can start with Honeybadger for free. Pricing model: Free tier with paid plans.

### Can I self-host Sentry or Honeybadger?

Sentry can be self-hosted. Deployment options: self-hosted or managed cloud. Honeybadger is offered as a managed service: managed cloud.

### What are the main drawbacks of Sentry and Honeybadger?

Sentry: event quotas require tuning; self-hosting is resource-heavy. Honeybadger: smaller feature set than Sentry; fewer integrations.
