# GitLab CI vs Jenkins: Head-to-Head Comparison

## Quick Verdict

> GitLab CI is the better pick for teams using GitLab for source control. Jenkins is the better pick for teams that need maximum control over self-hosted CI.

## Detailed Breakdown

### GitLab CI

_CI/CD integrated into the GitLab DevSecOps platform_

**Pros:**

- Tightly integrated with GitLab
- Advanced pipelines with DAGs and child pipelines
- Self-hostable runners and instances

**Cons:**

- Best experience requires GitLab
- Advanced features in paid tiers

---

### Jenkins

_Extensible open-source automation server_

**Pros:**

- Thousands of plugins
- Completely free and self-hosted
- Runs almost any workload

**Cons:**

- Significant maintenance overhead
- Dated UI and plugin compatibility issues

---

## Key Differences

- **Positioning:** GitLab CI — CI/CD integrated into the GitLab DevSecOps platform. Jenkins — extensible open-source automation server.
- Licensing differs: GitLab CI is open-core while Jenkins is open source.
- **Deployment:** GitLab CI — self-hosted or managed cloud. Jenkins — self-hosted.
- **Pricing:** GitLab CI — free tier with paid plans. Jenkins — free and open source.
- **Signature strength:** GitLab CI — tightly integrated with GitLab. Jenkins — thousands of plugins.

It depends on your requirements. GitLab CI is a strong fit for teams using GitLab for source control, while Jenkins suits teams that need maximum control over self-hosted CI.

Yes, you can start with GitLab CI for free. Pricing model: Free tier with paid plans.

### Is Jenkins free to use?

Yes, you can start with Jenkins for free. Pricing model: Free and open source.

### Can I self-host GitLab CI or Jenkins?

GitLab CI can be self-hosted. Deployment options: self-hosted or managed cloud. Jenkins can be self-hosted. Deployment options: self-hosted.

### What are the main drawbacks of GitLab CI and Jenkins?

GitLab CI: best experience requires GitLab; advanced features in paid tiers. Jenkins: significant maintenance overhead; dated UI and plugin compatibility issues.
