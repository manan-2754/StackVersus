## Quick Verdict

> Airtable is the industry leader for teams needing advanced automation, AI features, and a polished UI. Baserow is the superior choice for developers and privacy-conscious teams who prefer an open-source, self-hosted solution with transparent pricing.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Airtable

_The platform to build powerful apps on your data_

**Pros:**

- Extensive library of third-party integrations
- Advanced AI and automation capabilities
- Highly polished and intuitive user interface

**Cons:**

- Expensive at scale for large teams
- Proprietary platform with no self-hosting option
- Strict row limits on lower-tier plans

---

### Baserow

_The open-source no-code database tool_

**Pros:**

- Open-source and self-hostable on your own infrastructure
- No artificial row limits on self-hosted instances
- Highly customizable via API and plugins

**Cons:**

- Smaller ecosystem of third-party integrations
- Less advanced built-in automation features
- Requires technical knowledge for self-hosting maintenance

---

## Key Differences

- Deployment: Airtable is strictly SaaS, while Baserow offers both cloud and self-hosted deployment.
- Pricing: Airtable charges per user with strict feature gating; Baserow offers a more affordable self-hosted model with fewer restrictions.
- Extensibility: Airtable excels in pre-built integrations, whereas Baserow is designed for developers to extend via its open-source codebase.
- Feature Set: Airtable provides more mature 'no-code' features like AI-generated fields and complex interface builders.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I migrate from Airtable to Baserow?

Yes, Baserow provides a built-in importer that allows you to migrate your data and structure from Airtable easily.

### Which tool is better for data privacy?

Baserow is superior for privacy because you can host it on your own servers, ensuring your data never leaves your infrastructure.

### Is Baserow as easy to use as Airtable?

Airtable has a more refined, user-friendly interface, while Baserow is functional but slightly more utilitarian in its design.
